Let's: suggestions and wishes

Gidelim mi?

This unit adds two moods for what the speaker wants to happen. The optative says let me and let's: Kapıyı açayım mı? (Shall I open the door?), Gidelim mi? (Shall we go?). The third-person imperative says let him and let them: Ali gelsin (Let Ali come). On these forms rest the wishes Turks say every day, from Kolay gelsin to Geçmiş olsun, and the little word that gets things moving: hadi.

01Gidelim mi? Let's, let me, shall I

The optative (istek kipi) is the speaker's own wish or suggestion. In today's Turkish you use it in the first person: -(y)AyIm for let me, shall I, and -(y)AlIm for let's, shall we.

Optative, first person
let me:  stem + -(y)AyIm   geleyim
let's:   stem + -(y)AlIm   gelelim

The A is e or a by two-way harmony, the I follows four-way harmony: geleyim, gelelim; yapayım, yapalım; göreyim, görelim; duralım. After a stem that ends in a vowel, a buffer y comes first: okuyayım, okuyalım; bekleyeyim, bekleyelim; başlayalım.

Negative. -mA comes first, then a buffer y: gelmeyeyim (let me not come), gelmeyelim (let's not come), yapmayalım.

Questions. Put mI after the verb. Unlike the tenses with type I endings, nothing moves: the person is already part of the form. Geleyim mi? (Shall I come?), Gidelim mi? (Shall we go?). With a question word, there is no mI: Ne yapalım? (What shall we do?)

Stems change as they do before the future -(y)AcAk (unit 3): git- and et- soften to gid- and ed-, and de- and ye- become diy- and yiy-.

Uses. Gidelim suggests; Gidelim mi? suggests more gently, as a question. Kapıyı açayım mı? offers to do something, and Ne yapayım? asks for advice. On its own, the let me form announces what you are about to do: Bir bakayım (Let me have a look), and in a café Ben bir çay alayım (I'll have a tea).

Examples
  • Bu akşam sinemaya gidelim mi?Shall we go to the cinema this evening?
  • Kapıyı açayım mı?Shall I open the door?
  • Size yardım edeyim.Let me help you.
  • Hafta sonu ne yapalım?What shall we do at the weekend?
  • Yağmur yağıyor, dışarı çıkmayalım.It's raining; let's not go out.
  • Ben de bir çay alayım.I'll have a tea too.

The other persons. The optative also has forms for you, he and they: gelesin, gele, gelesiniz, geleler. They survive in old texts, folk songs and a few fixed sayings, but everyday Turkish uses the imperative instead: gel and gelin for you (A1 unit 12), and gelsin and gelsinler for he and they, in the next section.

Watch out

Gidelim (let's go) is not gideriz (we go, we'll go: the aorist of unit 4) or gideceğiz (we're going to go). Only the optative suggests.

Compare

Compare two offers. Çay içer misiniz? (unit 4) asks what the guest would like; Çay getireyim mi? (Shall I bring some tea?) offers to act. Both are polite, and hosts use both.

02Ali gelsin: let him, let them

The imperative of A1 unit 12 speaks to you. Turkish also has an imperative for he, she, it and they: the stem + -sIn, and -sInlAr for they. Ali gelsin means let Ali come, Ali should come or tell Ali to come: the speaker wants it, and someone else is to do it.

Third-person imperative
o:      stem + -sIn     gelsin, otursun
onlar:  stem + -sInlAr  gelsinler

The suffix starts with a consonant, so stems never change: gitsin, etsin, desin, yesin. Its vowel follows four-way harmony: gelsin, yapsın, okusun, görsün. With a plural subject, -lAr can be left out: Çocuklar yatsın or Çocuklar yatsınlar.

Questions ask for a decision about someone else: Ayşe de gelsin mi? (Should Ayşe come too? Shall I tell her to come?). The answer often repeats the verb: Gelsin (Sure, let her come).

Examples
  • Çocuklar erken yatsın.The children should go to bed early.
  • Ayşe de gelsin mi?Should Ayşe come too?
  • Bulaşıkları Mehmet yıkasın.Mehmet can do the dishes.
  • Misafirler salonda otursunlar.The guests can sit in the living room.
  • Kimse geç kalmasın.Nobody should be late.

Two everyday words are forms of olmak: Olsun (never mind, so be it, it doesn't matter) and its negative in Neden olmasın? (Why not?, literally why shouldn't it be?).

Watch out

-sIn after a bare stem is the imperative: gelsin, let him come. After a tense or a noun it is the ending for you: gelirsin (you come), yorgunsun (you're tired).

Tip

Instructions for a group often use it: Herkes kemerini taksın (Everyone should fasten their seat belt). And a host making plans: Sofrayı çocuklar kursun (The children can set the table).

03Kolay gelsin: a wish for every moment

Many everyday formulas are third-person imperatives of olmak or gelmek: wishes that something may be so. Each belongs to a moment, and leaving it out where Turks expect it can sound cold. Learn each one with its moment and its answer.

WishLiterallyWhenAnswer
Kolay gelsin.May it come easy.to anyone at work: a shopkeeper, a builder, a colleague at a deskSağ ol. Sağ olun.
Geçmiş olsun.May it be past.to someone who is ill, has had an accident or bad luckSağ ol. Teşekkür ederim.
Afiyet olsun.May it be health.to someone eating or about to eat; the cook's answer to thanksTeşekkürler.
Elinize sağlık.Health to your hands.to the cook after a meal, or to anyone who made something for youAfiyet olsun.
Hayırlı olsun.May it bring good.for something new: a job, a home, a car, a babyTeşekkürler.
Güle güle kullanın.Use it with laughter.for something someone has just boughtSağ olun.
Güle güle oturun.Live in it with laughter.for a new homeSağ olun.
İyi yolculuklar.Good journeys.to someone setting off on a tripTeşekkürler.
Başınız sağ olsun.May your head be well.to someone who has lost a relativeDostlar sağ olsun.

Most of these are the -sIn of the last section: ol-sun, gel-sin. Elinize sağlık has no verb at all: sağlık (health) goes to your hands, elinize, in the dative. Güle güle is the goodbye of A1 unit 1: it comes from gülmek, to laugh, and means with a smile.

The forms with -InIz and -In are for people you call siz. To a friend, say Eline sağlık, Güle güle kullan, Güle güle otur. The wishes in olsun don't change.

Examples
  • Kolay gelsin, Murat Bey!Hope the work's going well, Murat Bey!
  • Geçmiş olsun, şimdi nasılsın?Sorry to hear that. How are you now?
  • Yemek çok güzeldi, elinize sağlık.The meal was delicious, thank you.
  • Yeni bir iş buldum. — Hayırlı olsun!I've found a new job. — Congratulations!
  • İyi yolculuklar, bize mesaj at!Have a good trip, send us a message!
Culture

Kolay gelsin is said to strangers too: a waiter, a cashier, workers in the street, the person who comes to fix your heating. It costs nothing and opens every door.

Watch out

Geçmiş olsun is only for bad news, once it has happened. Before an exam or an interview, wish someone Başarılar (good luck, literally successes) or Kolay gelsin.

04Hadi gidelim! Come on

Hadi, or haydi in the dictionary and in careful speech, pushes someone to act: come on, let's go, go on. It goes with the optative, the imperative and the third-person imperative, and takes no endings of its own.

TurkishEnglish
Hadi gidelim!Come on, let's go!
Haydi çocuklar, kalkın!Come on, children, get up!
Hadi, sen başla.Go on, you start.
Hadi, o da gelsin.All right, he can come too.
Hadi bakalım!Come on then! Off we go!
Hadi canım!Come off it! You don't say!

Besides urging, hadi can accept something a little reluctantly (Hadi, o da gelsin: oh, all right, let him come too) and, in Hadi canım!, say that you don't believe what you've just heard. Bakalım, in Hadi bakalım, is itself an optative: let's see, from bakmak.

Examples
  • Hadi, acele edin, film başlıyor!Come on, hurry up, the film's starting!
  • Hava çok güzel, hadi sahile inelim.The weather is lovely; come on, let's go down to the seaside.
  • Hadi bakalım, herkes arabaya binsin.Come on then, everybody get in the car.
  • Hadi, bir çay daha içelim.Come on, let's have one more tea.
Watch out

Hadi is friendly but direct. With strangers or older people, a polite question is softer: Gelir misiniz? (unit 4). And to a group you change the verb, not hadi: Hadi gelin!

Tip

A plan agreed over the phone often ends with Anlaştık (Deal, agreed) and Hadi görüşürüz (Right, see you).

Summary

  1. Let me: stem + -(y)AyIm (geleyim, okuyayım); let's: stem + -(y)AlIm (gelelim, okuyalım). Negative gelmeyelim; question Gelelim mi?, with nothing moving onto mI.
  2. Stems change as before the future: gidelim, edeyim, diyelim, yiyelim.
  3. Offers and suggestions: Kapıyı açayım mı?, Gidelim mi?, Ne yapalım?
  4. Let him, let them: stem + -sIn, -sInlAr (gelsin, gelsinler); negative gelmesin; question Gelsin mi? The stem never changes.
  5. Olsun (never mind) and Neden olmasın? (Why not?) are forms of olmak.
  6. Set wishes: Kolay gelsin (at work), Geçmiş olsun (illness, bad luck), Afiyet olsun (eating), Elinize sağlık (to the cook), Hayırlı olsun (something new), İyi yolculuklar (a trip).
  7. Hadi or haydi: come on, with the optative and the imperatives: Hadi gidelim!
