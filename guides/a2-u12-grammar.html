Word building

İstanbullu bir gazeteci

Turkish makes new words by adding suffixes to old ones, and you have met many already: Kanadalı, sessiz, aşçı, gözlük, Türkçe. This unit names six of the most useful word-building suffixes (yapım ekleri): -lI (with, from), -sIz (without), -CI (the person who makes, sells or does), -lIk (-ness, a thing for, lasting), -CA (languages, manner, opinion) and the distributive -(ş)Ar (each), with -leyin for times of day. Each makes a new word, which then takes the usual endings: gözlüğüm, gazeteciler, Türkçeyi.

01Şekerli, İstanbullu: -lI

-lI turns a noun into an adjective meaning with, having, containing. It follows four-way harmony, and its l never changes: şekerli (with sugar, sweetened), sütlü (with milk), tuzlu (salty), bulutlu (cloudy), akıllı (clever: with brains).

On a place name, -lI means from: İstanbullu (from Istanbul), Ankaralı, İzmirli, and the Kanadalı of A1 unit 3. It makes a new word, so there is no apostrophe: İstanbullu, not İstanbul'lu. Nereli? asks from where?: Nerelisin? — İzmirliyim.

Like any adjective, a word in -lI goes before its noun: sütlü kahve (coffee with milk), bahçeli bir ev (a house with a garden), iki çocuklu bir aile (a family with two children). It can also be the predicate: Bugün hava bulutlu. Some old ones have their own meaning: tatlı (sweet), ünlü (famous: with fame), evli (married).

Examples
  • Çayı şekerli mi içiyorsun?Do you take your tea with sugar?
  • Bir sütlü kahve, lütfen.A coffee with milk, please.
  • Bu çorba çok tuzlu.This soup is very salty.
  • Bahçeli bir ev arıyoruz.We're looking for a house with a garden.
  • Ben İstanbulluyum, eşim Ankaralı.I'm from Istanbul; my husband is from Ankara.
Watch out

Şekerli and şekerle are not the same. Şekerli çay is a kind of tea, sweetened: -lI describes the noun. Şekerle, the -(y)lA of A1 unit 12, says what you use: Çayı şekerle içiyorum (I drink my tea with sugar). Before a noun, only -lI works: şekerli bir çay.

Tip

-lI also names supporters of a club: Galatasaraylı, Fenerbahçeli, Beşiktaşlı. Hangi takımlısın? asks which team you support.

02Şekersiz, işsiz: -sIz

-sIz is the opposite of -lI: without, -less. It follows four-way harmony, and its s never changes: şekersiz (without sugar), sütsüz (without milk), tuzsuz (unsalted), susuz (without water), işsiz (unemployed: without work), parasız (free of charge; broke), and the sessiz (quiet: without sound) of A1 unit 12.

With -lIEnglishWith -sIzEnglish

Words in -sIz work as adjectives (şekersiz bir kahve), as predicates with the personal endings (Kardeşim işsiz, Bugün parasızım: I'm broke today) and sometimes as adverbs: Çocuklar müzeye parasız giriyor (Children get into the museum for free).

Examples
  • Şekersiz bir çay, lütfen.A tea without sugar, please.
  • Kardeşim iki aydır işsiz.My brother has been out of work for two months.
  • Susuz yaşayamayız.We can't live without water.
  • Bugün müzeler parasız.The museums are free today.
  • Kahvemi sütsüz içerim.I drink my coffee without milk.
Tip

For a Turkish coffee, without sugar is sade (plain), as the A1 interlude explains; şekersiz is for tea and other coffees.

Watch out

-sIz is a word-building suffix, not the negative of a verb. I don't take sugar is Şeker almıyorum; şekersiz describes the drink.

03Gazeteci, kitapçı: -CI

-CI names the person who makes, sells, repairs or does something, and often the shop as well: gazeteci (journalist), kitapçı (bookseller; bookshop), çiçekçi (florist), futbolcu (footballer). It follows four-way harmony, and its C is c after a vowel or a voiced consonant, ç after a voiceless one (f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p), the same rule as the d and t of A1 unit 5.

The stem never softens before -CI, which starts with a consonant: kitapçı, çiçekçi, not kitabçı. The shop and the person share the word: Kitapçıya gidiyorum (I'm going to the bookshop). You know aşçı (cook) and kapıcı (caretaker) already; kuyumcu (jeweller) and halıcı (carpet seller) appear in the interlude, where whole streets are named with -CI in the plural.

Some -CI words describe a habit rather than a trade: yalancı (liar), şakacı (joker, someone who likes to joke).

Examples
  • Ablam gazeteci.My older sister is a journalist.
  • Kitapçı postanenin karşısında.The bookshop is opposite the post office.
  • Çiçekçiden güzel çiçekler aldım.I bought some lovely flowers at the florist's.
  • Balıkçılar sabah erken denize çıkar.Fishermen go out to sea early in the morning.
  • Yolcular otobüse bindi.The passengers got on the bus.
Compare

-CI is close to English -er (baker) and French -ier (boulanger), but it names the shop too: kitapçı is the bookseller and the bookshop, as French libraire and librairie are almost one word.

04Güzellik, gözlük, bir haftalık: -lIk

-lIk follows four-way harmony and has three jobs.

  1. Abstract nouns, like English -ness, -hood, -ship: güzellik (beauty), iyilik (goodness, a kindness), gençlik (youth), arkadaşlık (friendship), hastalık (illness), and the çocukluk (childhood) of unit 2.
  2. A thing for something: gözlük (glasses: for the eyes), sözlük (dictionary: for words), kitaplık (bookcase), tuzluk (salt shaker), kulaklık (headphones: for the ears).
  3. After a number and a measure, an adjective of duration, size or value: bir haftalık tatil (a week's holiday), iki saatlik yol (a two-hour journey), dört kişilik masa (a table for four), elli liralık bilet (a fifty-lira ticket).

Its k softens to ğ before a vowel, like the k of most longer words (A1 unit 8): gözlüğüm (my glasses), güzelliği (its beauty), kitaplığa (to the bookcase). And the suffixes stack: işsizlik (unemployment: the state of being without work).

Examples
  • Gözlüğümü bulamıyorum.I can't find my glasses.
  • Bir haftalık tatile gidiyoruz.We're going on a week's holiday.
  • Dört kişilik bir masa, lütfen.A table for four, please.
  • Kitaplar kitaplıkta.The books are in the bookcase.
  • Bu iyiliği unutmam.I won't forget this kindness.
Watch out

Bir haftalık describes a noun: bir haftalık tatil. After a verb, for a week is just bir hafta: Bir hafta kaldık (We stayed for a week).

Tip

Ne var ne yok? (What's new?) is often answered İyilik, sağlık (All is well: goodness, health).

05Türkçe, yavaşça, bence: -CA

-CA follows two-way harmony (-ca, -ce), and its C becomes ç after a voiceless consonant (-ça, -çe). It has three everyday jobs.

Languages are the people + -CA: Türkçe, İngilizce, Fransızca, Almanca, İspanyolca, Rusça, Arapça, Japonca. They take endings with no apostrophe: Türkçeyi, İngilizcede (in English).

Manner. On an adjective, -CA makes an adverb with a touch of care: yavaşça (slowly, gently), sessizce (quietly), iyice (thoroughly). The bare adjective of unit 8 is the everyday choice (yavaş konuş); -CA adds softness or care.

Opinion. On a pronoun, -CA means in … opinion: bence (in my opinion), sence (in your opinion), bizce, sizce. Alone, Sence? and Sizce? ask What do you think? Bence is the everyday twin of the bana göre of unit 9.

Examples
  • Bence bu film çok güzel.In my opinion this film is very good.
  • Sizce hangisi daha iyi?Which one do you think is better?
  • Kapıyı yavaşça kapattı.She closed the door gently.
  • Arkadaşım Rusça ve Japonca biliyor.My friend speaks Russian and Japanese.
  • Saatlerce seni bekledim!I waited for you for hours!
Tip

On a plural number or time word, -CA means by the …: yüzlerce (hundreds of), binlerce (thousands of), saatlerce (for hours), yıllarca (for years).

Watch out

Language names take their endings without an apostrophe: Türkçede, İngilizceyi, never Türkçe'de. They keep the capital of the people's name: Türkçe, İngilizce.

06Birer, ikişer; sabahleyin: each, and times of day

On a number, -(ş)Ar means each, apiece: Çocuklara birer elma verdim (I gave the children an apple each). It follows two-way harmony and takes ş after a vowel: ikişer, altışar, yedişer. The t of dört softens: dörder.

Doubled, they mean in groups of: birer birer (one by one), ikişer ikişer (two by two). Kaçar? asks how many each? or how much each?: Biletler kaçar lira? (How much is each ticket?)

-leyin. Four time words take -leyin, which never harmonizes: sabahleyin (in the morning), öğleyin (at noon, from öğle, noon), akşamleyin (in the evening), geceleyin (at night). They sound a little old-fashioned or written; speech also uses the bare words: Sabah erken kalktım.

Examples
  • Çocuklara birer dondurma aldım.I bought the children an ice cream each.
  • Herkes ikişer kitap aldı.Everyone bought two books each.
  • Öğrenciler sınıfa birer birer girdi.The students came into the classroom one by one.
  • Biletler kaçar lira?How much is each ticket?
  • Sabahleyin erken kalkarım.I get up early in the morning.
Watch out

Birer is not bir. Çocuklara bir elma verdim is one apple for all the children; birer elma is one apple each.

Compare

English needs each or apiece after the noun, and French chacun: they got two books each, ils ont eu deux livres chacun. Turkish marks the number: ikişer kitap.

Summary

  1. -lI means with or from: şekerli, bulutlu, İstanbullu (no apostrophe). -sIz means without: şekersiz, işsiz, susuz.
  2. -CI names who makes, sells or does something, and often the shop: gazeteci, kitapçı; c after vowels and voiced consonants, ç after voiceless ones.
  3. -lIk makes abstract nouns (güzellik), things for something (gözlük, kitaplık) and measures (bir haftalık, dört kişilik); its k softens: gözlüğüm.
  4. -CA makes languages (Türkçe, Rusça), adverbs of manner (yavaşça) and opinions (bence, sence).
  5. -(ş)Ar on a number means each: birer, ikişer, dörder; birer birer is one by one.
  6. -leyin works on four time words only: sabahleyin, öğleyin, akşamleyin, geceleyin.
