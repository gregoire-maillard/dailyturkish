The past tense

Dün ne yaptın?

Every verb so far has been in the present continuous. This unit adds the past tense -DI, the one you use for things that happened and are over: Dün ne yaptın? (What did you do yesterday?) Sinemaya gittim (I went to the cinema). It brings a second set of personal endings, which you'll compare with the first, and the time words that go with the past.

01Geldim: the past tense -DI

The past tense (görülen geçmiş zaman, the witnessed past) is built in three pieces: the stem, -DI, and a personal ending: gel- + -di + -m → geldim (I came). It tells what happened, usually something you saw or did yourself.

Past tense
stem + -DI + -m / -n / -∅ / -k / -nIz / -lAr
D → d   after vowels and voiced consonants
D → t   after f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p
I       four-way harmony with the stem

The D behaves exactly like the D of the locative in A1 unit 5: geldi, okudu, but yaptı, içti, gitti. The I follows four-way harmony with the last vowel of the stem: geldi, aldı, okudu, gördü.

The stem never changes. Because -DI starts with a consonant, nothing softens and no vowel drops, unlike with -(I)yor: gitti (but gidiyor), bekledi (but bekliyor), yedi (but yiyor), dedi (but diyor).

Examples
  • Dün akşam film izledim.Yesterday evening I watched a film.
  • Ayşe annesine bir mektup yazdı.Ayşe wrote a letter to her mother.
  • Hafta sonu çok güzel bir kitap okudum.At the weekend I read a very good book.
  • Dün sinemaya gittik.We went to the cinema yesterday.
  • Hafta sonu ne yaptın?What did you do at the weekend?
  • Ne dedin?What did you say?
Watch out

The ending for we is -k, not -(y)Iz: geldik, yaptık. And you (plural or polite) is -nIz: geldiniz, never geldisiniz. You'll see why in the third section.

Tip

I'm hungry: acıktım

Some changes of state are said in the past, where English uses the present: Acıktım (I'm hungry: I have got hungry), Susadım (I'm thirsty), Yoruldum (I'm tired), Geç kaldım (I'm late), Anladım (I see, got it), Bitti (It's over).

02Gelmedim, geldin mi? Negative and questions

Negative. Put -mA between the stem and -DI: gel- + -me- + -di + -m → gelmedim (I didn't come). This -mA has two-way harmony, e or a, unlike the -mI of gelmiyor. Since it ends in a vowel, the D after it is always d: gitmedi, yapmadı.

Questions. Here the past works the other way round from -(I)yor: the personal ending stays on the verb, and mI comes last, as a separate word in harmony with the vowel before it: Geldin mi? (Did you come?), Okudun mu?, Gördün mü?, Yaptınız mı? Compare Geliyor musun?, where the ending moves onto the particle.

Examples
  • Yemek yedin mi?Have you eaten? Did you eat?
  • Filmi gördünüz mü?Did you see the film?
  • Ayşe gelmedi.Ayşe didn't come.
  • Anlamadım.I didn't understand. Sorry, I didn't catch that.
  • Mektubu okumadın mı?Didn't you read the letter?
  • Otobüsü kaçırdılar mı?Did they miss the bus?

A short answer repeats the verb: Geldin mi? Evet, geldim. or Hayır, gelmedim.

Watch out

Never move the ending onto the particle with -DI: geldin mi?, not geldi misin?; yaptınız mı?, not yaptı mısınız? For they, -lAr stays on the verb too: geldiler mi?

Compare

English needs did for questions and negatives, and often uses have for recent events. Turkish -DI covers them all: Yemek yedin mi? is both Did you eat? and Have you eaten?, and Anahtarımı kaybettim is I lost my key or I've lost my key.

03Two sets of personal endings

Turkish verbs take one of two main sets of personal endings. You learned the first in A1 unit 3 with to be and used it in A1 unit 6 with -(I)yor. The past tense brings the second. Turkish grammars call them zamir kökenli (from the pronouns, as -(y)Iz recalls biz) and iyelik kökenli (from the possessive suffixes); English-language grammars say type I and type II, or the z-type and the k-type, after their endings for we.

Which forms take which set
EndingsForms that take them
Type Ipresent of to be (öğrenciyim), present continuous (geliyorum), and later the future (geleceğim), the aorist (gelirim), -mIş (gelmişim) and -mAlI (gelmeliyim)
Type IIpast -DI (geldim), and later the past of to be (öğrenciydim) and the conditional (gelsem)

Two things help you keep them apart. Type II endings look like the possessive suffixes of A1 unit 9: evim, evin and geldim, geldin. And in a question, type I endings move onto mI (geliyor musun?), while type II endings stay on the verb (geldin mi?).

Examples
  • Çalışıyoruz.We're working.
  • Çalıştık.We worked.
  • Hazır mısınız?Are you ready?
  • Eve geldiniz mi?Did you get home?
  • Yorgunum.I'm tired.
  • Yoruldum.I'm tired. I've got tired.
Watch out

Don't mix the two sets on one verb: geldik, not geldiyiz; geldiniz, not geldisiniz; geldin, not geldisin.

Tip

The imperative of A1 unit 12 has persons of its own (gel, gelin), and so will the optative and the third-person imperative later in A2. Learn each set with the form that takes it.

04Yesterday, last week, two days ago

These time words go with the past. Like all time words, they usually come at the start of the sentence or right after the subject.

… önce, ago. Put önce after a number and a time word: üç gün önce, beş dakika önce, on yıl önce. After a number the noun stays singular, as in A1 unit 11: iki gün önce, never iki günler önce. (Önce after the ablative, yemekten önce, means before; that comes in unit 9.)

Not yet: henüz, daha. With a negative verb, henüz and daha both mean not yet; daha is more colloquial: Henüz gelmedi or Daha gelmedi (He hasn't come yet). With a positive verb, henüz means just: Henüz geldim (I've only just arrived).

Already: zaten. Zaten means already, or anyway when it backs up what you say: Zaten yedim (I've already eaten), Zaten geç kaldık (We're late anyway).

Examples
  • Geçen hafta İzmir'e gittik.Last week we went to Izmir.
  • Ali iki saat önce aradı.Ali called two hours ago.
  • Tren henüz gelmedi.The train hasn't come yet.
  • Daha kahvaltı yapmadım.I haven't had breakfast yet.
  • Biz zaten yedik.We've already eaten.
  • Dün gece çok geç uyudum.I went to sleep very late last night.
Compare

English puts not yet and already with the present perfect: I haven't eaten yet, I've already eaten. Turkish keeps the ordinary past, much as French uses its passé composé for both I ate and I have eaten: Henüz yemedim, Zaten yedim.

Watch out

Önce follows the time phrase, like English ago: iki gün önce, not önce iki gün. And geçen comes before the noun: geçen hafta, last week.

Summary

  1. The past tense is stem + -DI + a type II ending: geldim, geldin, geldi, geldik, geldiniz, geldiler.
  2. D becomes t after f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p (yaptım, gitti), and the I follows four-way harmony (aldı, okudu, gördü).
  3. The stem never changes before -DI: gitti, bekledi, yedi.
  4. Negative -mA before -DI: gelmedim, yapmadık.
  5. In a past question the ending stays on the verb and mI comes last: Geldin mi?, Yaptınız mı?
  6. Type I endings (-(y)Im, -sIn, -(y)Iz, -sInIz) go with to be and -(I)yor; type II (-m, -n, -k, -nIz) with -DI.
  7. Time words: dün, geçen hafta, iki gün önce; henüz or daha + negative for not yet; zaten for already.
