Passive and reflexive

1557'de tamamlandı

Turkish changes the voice of a verb with a suffix placed right after the stem, before any tense or ending. This unit adds two such suffixes: the passive (yapıldı, it was built), which also makes the impersonal passive for rules and general statements (Burada sigara içilmez, No smoking here), and the reflexive (giyindi, she got dressed). It ends with kendi, self, for everything the reflexive suffix can't say.

01Yapıldı: the passive

A passive verb tells what happened to someone or something, usually without saying who did it: Cami 1557'de tamamlandı (The mosque was completed in 1557). The passive suffix comes right after the stem, and every tense, ending and form of the earlier units can follow it: yapılıyor, yapıldı, yapılacak, yapılmış.

Stem changes. Gitmek and etmek soften before the vowel, as they do before any vowel: gidilmek, edilmek. So every verb made with etmek has its passive in edilmek: davet edildim (I was invited), inşa edildi (it was built). Yemek and demek take -n: yenmek, denmek; söylemek gives söylenmek.

A passive stem always has more than one syllable, so its aorist is -Ir: yapılır, okunur, bulunur, never yapılar.

The object becomes the subject, so it loses its accusative: Mektubu yazdım (I wrote the letter) → Mektup yazıldı (The letter was written).

Who did it. Most passive sentences leave the doer out. To name it, put tarafından (by) after the noun: Süleymaniye Mimar Sinan tarafından yapıldı (The Süleymaniye was built by Mimar Sinan). It sounds formal, at home in history books, the news and official letters; in conversation Turkish prefers the active: Süleymaniye'yi Mimar Sinan yaptı. With a pronoun it takes the genitive: benim tarafımdan, onun tarafından.

Participles and -mIş adjectives are built on passive verbs too: 1557'de tamamlanan cami (the mosque completed in 1557), kırılmış bardak (a broken glass), haşlanmış yumurta (a boiled egg), the words B1 unit 3 promised.

Examples
  • Cami 1557'de tamamlandı.The mosque was completed in 1557.
  • Cüzdanım bulundu!My wallet has been found!
  • Bu köprü 16. yüzyılda yapıldı.This bridge was built in the 16th century.
  • Düğüne davet edildik.We were invited to the wedding.
  • Toplantı yarına ertelendi.The meeting has been postponed until tomorrow.
  • Bu kitap çok okunuyor.This book is widely read.
Watch out

After a stem in l the suffix is -In, not -Il: bulundu, alındı, bilinir. Bululdu doesn't exist.

Tip

Bulunmak

Bulunmak has a life of its own. Besides to be found, it means to be (somewhere) in careful Turkish: Müze şehir merkezinde bulunuyor (The museum is in the city centre). Many other passives also serve as plain intransitive verbs: Kapı açıldı (The door opened), Ders bitti, okul kapandı.

02Burada sigara içilmez: rules and general statements

Turkish also makes a passive from verbs without an object, which English can't do: gidilir (literally it is gone), oturulur, girilmez. The result has no subject at all and speaks of people in general: Oraya otobüsle gidilir (You get there by bus). This is the impersonal passive.

In the aorist it states a rule, a custom or the right way to do something: Burada yüzülmez (No swimming here), Camiye ayakkabıyla girilmez (You don't go into a mosque with your shoes on). An ordinary passive with a general subject does the same job: Burada sigara içilmez (No smoking here), Türkiye'de misafire çay ikram edilir (In Turkey, guests are offered tea).

Questions ask how things are done: Oraya nasıl gidilir? (How do you get there?), Bu kelime nasıl yazılır? (How is this word spelled?), Bu nasıl okunur? (How do you pronounce this?), Buna Türkçede ne denir? (What's this called in Turkish?).

Recipes and instructions give their steps in the passive aorist: Önce soğanlar doğranır ve yağa eklenir (First the onions are chopped and added to the oil). Su kaynayınca yumurtalar eklenir (When the water boils, the eggs go in).

The doer is everyone, so tarafından never appears, and the verb stays in the third person singular.

Examples
  • Oraya nasıl gidilir?How do you get there?
  • Burada yüzülmez.No swimming here.
  • Bu kelime nasıl yazılır?How is this word spelled?
  • Buna Türkçede ne denir?What do you call this in Turkish?
  • Bayramda büyüklerin eli öpülür.At bayram, people kiss the hands of their elders.
  • Önce soğanlar doğranır.First the onions are chopped.
Tip

Denir and yenir

Demek and yemek give denir (it is said, it's called; also denilir) and yenir (it is eaten, it's edible): Bu mantar yenir mi? (Is this mushroom edible?).

Watch out

An impersonal passive has no person: Oraya otobüsle gidilir, never gidiliriz. To include yourself, use the active: Oraya otobüsle gideriz (We go there by bus).

03Yıkanmak, giyinmek: the reflexive

A small group of verbs take -(I)n for an action you do to yourself (dönüşlü çatı): yıkamak (to wash) → yıkanmak (to have a wash), giymek (to put on) → giyinmek (to get dressed), hazırlamak (to prepare) → hazırlanmak (to get ready). After a vowel the suffix is just -n.

VerbReflexiveMeaning
yıkamakyıkanmakto wash (yourself), to have a wash or a bath
giymekgiyinmekto get dressed
soymaksoyunmakto get undressed
hazırlamakhazırlanmakto get ready
taramaktaranmakto comb your hair
kurulamakkurulanmakto dry yourself
övmekövünmekto boast, to show off

Passive or reflexive? After a vowel, and after l, the two suffixes look the same, so some forms have two readings: yıkandı can be he had a wash or it was washed. The subject decides: Çocuk yıkandı (The child had a wash, or was given one), but Araba yıkandı (The car was washed).

No object. A reflexive verb already contains its object. For one piece of clothing, use the plain verb: Ceketimi giydim (I put on my jacket), but Giyindim (I got dressed). The same goes for parts of the body: Ellerimi yıkadım (I washed my hands).

Examples
  • Hemen giyiniyorum, beş dakika bekle.I'm getting dressed right now; wait five minutes.
  • Düğün için hazırlanıyoruz.We're getting ready for the wedding.
  • Aynanın önünde tarandı.She combed her hair in front of the mirror.
  • Çocuklar yıkandı mı?Have the children had their bath?
  • Duş alıp giyindim.I had a shower and got dressed.
Compare

French marks these verbs with se: se laver, s'habiller, se préparer; Turkish has one suffix for the same idea: yıkanmak, giyinmek, hazırlanmak. But many French reflexives are plain verbs in Turkish: se lever is kalkmak, se promener is gezmek.

Tip

A few verbs with this suffix have meanings of their own today: görünmek (to seem, to look: Yorgun görünüyorsun, You look tired) and bulunmak (to be located).

04Kendi: self and own

For myself, yourself and the rest, Turkish uses kendi with a possessive ending, then whatever case the verb needs: Aynada kendime baktım (I looked at myself in the mirror), Kendini nasıl hissediyorsun? (How do you feel?). Most actions have no reflexive verb, so kendi is the usual way to say -self.

PersonAccusativeDativeAblative
Ikendimikendimekendimden
youkendinikendinekendinden

In the third person singular, the short forms kendini, kendine, kendinden are more common in speech than kendisini, kendisine, kendisinden, so kendini can mean yourself or himself, herself; context decides.

Kendi + noun: own. Before a noun, bare kendi means own, and the noun keeps its possessive ending: kendi evim (my own house), kendi arabası (her own car), Kendi odan var mı? (Do you have your own room?).

Myself, in person. Alone as a subject, kendim, kendin, kendisi stress who did it: Bu pastayı kendim yaptım (I made this cake myself), Müdürün kendisi geldi (The director came in person). Kendisi is also a polite way to refer to someone: Kendisi şu anda toplantıda (He's in a meeting at the moment).

Set phrases. Kendi kendine means by itself, on one's own or to oneself: Kapı kendi kendine açıldı (The door opened by itself), Kendi kendine konuşuyor (He's talking to himself). Kendi başına means on one's own, alone: Kendi başına mı geldin? (Did you come on your own?). And Kendine iyi bak (Take care of yourself) is an everyday goodbye.

Examples
  • Aynada kendime baktım.I looked at myself in the mirror.
  • Bugün kendimi çok yorgun hissediyorum.I feel very tired today.
  • Bu pastayı kendim yaptım.I made this cake myself.
  • Kendi odan var mı?Do you have your own room?
  • Kapı kendi kendine açıldı.The door opened by itself.
  • Kendine iyi bak!Take care of yourself!
Watch out

Use either the reflexive verb or kendi, not both: Yıkandım (I had a wash), not Kendimi yıkandım. And kendi before a noun needs the possessive on the noun: kendi evim, not kendi ev.

Summary

  1. The passive comes right after the stem: -Il after consonants (yapıldı), -In after l (bulundu), -n after vowels (okundu); gid-il-, ed-il-.
  2. The object of the active becomes the subject, without its accusative: Mektup yazıldı; tarafından names the doer, formally: Sinan tarafından yapıldı.
  3. Verbs without an object have an impersonal passive for people in general: Oraya nasıl gidilir?, Burada yüzülmez, Buna ne denir?
  4. Rules, customs and recipes use the passive aorist, -Ir in the positive: Soğanlar doğranır, Sigara içilmez.
  5. A few verbs take -(I)n as a reflexive and take no object: yıkanmak, giyinmek, soyunmak, hazırlanmak, taranmak.
  6. Kendi + possessive is myself, yourself…, with any case: kendimi, kendine; kendi + possessed noun is own: kendi evim.
  7. Kendi kendine (by itself, to oneself), kendi başına (on one's own), Kendine iyi bak! (Take care!).
