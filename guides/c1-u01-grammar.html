Stacked modality

Burada yaşamış olmalılar

From C1 on, verbs grow longer rather than newer. This unit stacks forms you already know: a participle such as -mIş, -(I)yor or -(y)AcAk, then a form of olmak that carries a modality, a tense or a condition. Gitmiş olmalı (she must have left), gitmiş olacak (she will have left), gidecek olsa (if she were to go), gitmiş olsaydı (if she had left). Archaeologists live on such guesses, so many of the examples come from a dig.

01Gitmiş olmalı: guesses about the past

In A2 unit 6 you used -mAlI for a confident guess about the present: Evde olmalı (She must be at home). To guess about the past, put the -mIş participle first and olmalı after it: Gitmiş olmalı (She must have left). The participle never changes; the person ending goes on olmalı.

verb + -mIş + olmalı + type I ending
git-  + miş + olmalı       → gitmiş olmalı
unut- + muş + olmalı + yım → unutmuş olmalıyım

The negative goes on the main verb, inside the guess: Mektubu okumamış olmalı (He must not have read the letter). The participle is then -mAmIş.

Questions. A question with olmalı sounds odd, because a guess is something you assert. To ask whether something could have happened, use olabilir mi: Yanlış anlamış olabilir miyim? (Could I have misunderstood?).

May have, can't have. Swap olmalı for the ability forms of A2 unit 5: -mIş olabilir (may have, might have) and -mIş olamaz (can't have). Together they make a small scale of confidence about the past.

FormExampleMeaning
-DIGitti.She left. (I know it.)
-mIş olmalıGitmiş olmalı.She must have left.
-mIş olabilirGitmiş olabilir.She may have left.
-mAmIş olabilirGitmemiş olabilir.She may not have left.
-mIş olamazGitmiş olamaz.She can't have left.

In progress. With -(I)yor instead of -mIş, the guess is about something going on right now: Telefonu açmıyor; duş alıyor olmalı (She isn't answering: she must be in the shower). Plain Uyumalı would mean he should sleep, so Uyuyor olmalı (he must be asleep) removes the doubt.

Examples
  • Işıklar yanıyor; çocuklar eve dönmüş olmalı.The lights are on: the children must have come home.
  • Anahtarı arabada unutmuş olabilirim.I may have left the key in the car.
  • Bu kadar kısa sürede bitirmiş olamazlar.They can't have finished in such a short time.
  • Mesajımı görmemiş olabilir.He may not have seen my message.
  • Telefonu açmıyor; duş alıyor olmalı.She isn't answering the phone: she must be in the shower.
Watch out

Gelmeliydi (B1 unit 2) is he should have come, and usually means he didn't. Gelmiş olmalı is he must have come, a conclusion that he did. The two look alike and say nearly opposite things.

Tip

Bare -mIş (B1 unit 3) already infers: looking at a wet street, Yağmur yağmış (It's rained). Yağmur yağmış olmalı adds your reasoning, it must have rained, and is the natural choice when you think aloud or argue from clues.

Compare

French builds the same guess with devoir: “elle a dû partir” is gitmiş olmalı, and “elle doit être chez elle” is evde olmalı.

02Bitirmiş olacağım: will have done, will be doing

To see a future action as already finished, use -mIş with olacak, the future of olmak: Saat altıda raporu bitirmiş olacağım (By six I'll have finished the report). Turkish needs no word for by: a time phrase, -DIğIndA or -(y)AnA kadar sets the moment.

verb + -mIş + olacak + type I ending
bitir- + miş + olacağ + ım → bitirmiş olacağım

Will be doing. With -(I)yor instead of -mIş, the action is in progress at that future moment: Yarın bu saatte uçakta uyuyor olacağım (This time tomorrow I'll be asleep on the plane).

Examples
  • Kazı bittiğinde üç evi tamamen kazmış olacağız.When the dig ends, we will have excavated three houses completely.
  • Sen gelene kadar yemeği hazırlamış olacağım.By the time you come, I'll have made dinner.
  • Haftaya bu saatte tatile çıkmış olacaklar.This time next week they'll have gone on holiday.
  • Eylülde raporu hâlâ yazıyor olacağım.In September I'll still be writing the report.
Tip

For a guess about now, they'll have arrived by now, Turkish prefers varmış olmalılar or the -DIr of unit 2: Şimdiye kadar varmışlardır.

Watch out

Customer-service language has spread -(I)yor olacak to plain promises: Sizi arıyor olacağız (We'll be calling you). Many speakers hear it as a calque of English and prefer the simple future, Sizi arayacağız. Keep -(I)yor olacak for an action in progress at a future moment.

03Gidecek olsam: if it came to that

B2 unit 10 gave -(y)AcAk olursa, if it should happen that. With olsa, the unreal conditional of olmak, the case becomes merely imagined or unlikely, and the main clause usually takes the aorist or -(I)rdI: Bir gün Türkiye'den taşınacak olsam en çok çayı özlerdim (If I were ever to move away from Turkey, I'd miss the tea most).

verb + -(y)AcAk + olsa + type II ending
taşın- + acak + olsa + m → taşınacak olsam
gel- + me + y + ecek + olsa + k → gelmeyecek olsak

There is no question form of the clause: the question goes on the main verb, Bunu satacak olsan kaça satardın? (If you were to sell this, how much would you sell it for?).

Five ways to say if you come
ClauseMeaning
Gelirsen…If you come… (a real possibility)
Gelecek olursan…If you should come, if you ever come…
Gelsen…If you came…
Gelecek olsan…If you were to come, if it came to your coming…
Gelseydin…If you had come…

The past. -(y)AcAk olsaydı imagines a past intention that never existed: Gelecek olsaydın bize haber verirdin (If you'd been going to come, you would have told us).

Was about to. The same pair in the plain past, -(y)AcAk oldu, means was about to, made as if to, and usually didn't: Bir şey söyleyecek oldu, sonra vazgeçti (He was about to say something, then changed his mind).

Examples
  • Bir gün buradan taşınacak olsam en çok bu manzarayı özlerim.If I were ever to move away from here, I'd miss this view most.
  • Yürüyerek gidecek olsak bile yetişiriz.Even if it came to walking there, we'd make it in time.
  • Bunu satacak olsan kaça satardın?If you were to sell this, how much would you sell it for?
  • Kapıyı açacak oldu, ama birden durdu.She was about to open the door, but suddenly stopped.
Watch out

The person ending goes on olsa, and the participle never takes one: gidecek olsam, never gideceğim olsa.

04Bilmiş olsaydım: the counterfactual perfect

You know the unreal past from B2 unit 1: Bilseydim gelirdim (If I had known, I would have come). Writing and careful speech often say it with -mIş olsaydı, which stresses a finished action: Daha erken başlamış olsaydık mezarı bulurduk (If we had started earlier, we would have found the grave). The two usually mean the same.

verb + -mIş + olsaydı + type II ending
başla- + mış + olsaydı + k → başlamış olsaydık

As with -sAydI, the if-clause has no question form; the question goes on the main verb: Bilmiş olsaydın gelir miydin? (Would you have come if you had known?). For they, olsaydılar is also heard.

The main clause takes -(I)rdI or -(y)AcAktI, as in B2, or -mIş olurdu (would have done), which matches the finished action: Erken çıkmış olsaydık treni yakalamış olurduk (If we had left early, we would have caught the train).

Wishes and regrets. After keşke (B2 unit 1): Keşke ona söylemiş olsaydım (I wish I had told him). Without the past -ydI, -mIş olsa looks at a present result: Şimdiye kadar varmış olsalar ararlardı (If they had arrived by now, they would have called).

FormExampleFeel
-sAydIBilseydim gelirdim.everyday
-mIş olsaydıBilmiş olsaydım gelirdim.careful or written; stresses the finished action
Examples
  • Haritayı daha dikkatli okumuş olsaydık kaybolmazdık.If we had read the map more carefully, we wouldn't have got lost.
  • Keşke o gün kazıya gitmiş olsaydım.I wish I had gone to the dig that day.
  • Bu tableti görmemiş olsaydı dili çözemezdi.If he hadn't seen this tablet, he couldn't have deciphered the language.
  • Erken çıkmış olsaydık treni yakalamış olurduk.If we had left early, we would have caught the train.

05Gelememiş olabilir: reading modal chains

C1 texts, academic ones above all, pile these forms on top of each other. Read them as you read the stacked voices of B2 unit 7: from the root out, one suffix, one meaning.

ChainRoot outMeaning
gelememiş olabilirgel- come → -eme- cannot → -miş → olabilir mayhe may not have been able to come
bulunamamış olmalıbul- find → -un- passive → -ama- cannot → -mış → olmalı mustit must not have been possible to find it
yürüyebilmiş olmalılaryürü- walk → -yebil- can → -miş → olmalılar they mustthey must have managed to walk
okumamış olabiliroku- read → -ma- not → -mış → olabilir mayhe may not have read it
görmüş olmayabilirgör- see → -müş → olmayabilir may notshe may not have seen it
yapılmış olması gerekiryap- do → -ıl- passive → -mış → olması gerekir it is necessary that it beit must have been done (formal)

May not have: two ways. The negative can sit on the main verb or on olmak, with the same meaning: Mesajı görmemiş olabilir and Mesajı görmüş olmayabilir both say she may not have seen the message. But Mesajı görmüş olamaz is she can't have seen it.

Could have, but didn't. Add the past to olabilir and the possibility becomes a missed one: Kaybolmuş olabilirdik (We could have got lost, but we didn't), against Kaybolmuş olabilirler (They may have got lost: we don't know yet). Compare -(y)AbilIrdI in B1 unit 2.

Formal guesses often turn the chain into a verbal noun (B1 unit 4) before gerek, muhtemel or mümkün: Taşların uzaktan getirilmiş olması gerekir (The stones must have been brought from far away), Kentin bir depremle yıkılmış olması muhtemeldir (The town was probably destroyed by an earthquake).

Examples
  • Mezarı bulamamış olabilirler.They may not have been able to find the grave.
  • Bu taşlar uzaktan getirilmiş olmalı.These stones must have been brought from far away.
  • Mesajı görmüş olmayabilir.She may not have seen the message.
  • Kaybolmuş olabilirdik.We could have got lost.
  • Taşların uzaktan getirilmiş olması gerekir.The stones must have been brought from far away.
Tip

Endings come last: the person ending and the question particle go on the final word of the chain. Gelmiş olmalılar, Gelmiş olabilir mi?, never gelmişler olmalı.

Summary

  1. -mIş olmalı is a confident guess about the past: Gitmiş olmalı (She must have left). -mIş olabilir is may have, -mIş olamaz can't have.
  2. -(I)yor olmalı guesses about something in progress: Uyuyor olmalı (He must be asleep).
  3. -mIş olacak is will have done and -(I)yor olacak will be doing; the person ending goes on olacak.
  4. -(y)AcAk olsa imagines an unlikely case: Taşınacak olsam… (If I were to move…). In the plain past, -(y)AcAk oldu is was about to.
  5. -mIş olsaydı is the unreal past with a finished action, usually the same as -sAydI; its main clause can be -mIş olurdu.
  6. Read chains from the root out; the person ending and mI go on the last word.
  7. Gelmiş olmalı (he must have come) is not gelmeliydi (he should have come).
